Superresolution.nl : Building a National Infrastructure

A national platform for super-resolution microscopy that did not exist before I arrived. The challenge was not the technology: it was the coordination: aligning three faculties within the university, bridging to six partner institutions across the Netherlands, and negotiating the governance that turned a collection of microscopes into a shared national facility. Today it operates as a federated infrastructure with open access calls, shared data standards, and a steering model that distributes decision-making across stakeholders rather than concentrating it in a single institution. The invisible work , the memos, the alignment meetings, the legal frameworks, is what made the visible facility possible.

National Research Infrastructure Proposals

Designing large-scale research infrastructure proposals for national funding programmes. The work spans the full lifecycle: from scientific case development and landscape analysis, through consortium building and governance modelling, to facility blueprinting and long-term sustainability planning. Each proposal is an exercise in institutional diplomacy: matching ambition to political reality, negotiating access models across organisations with different incentives, and building the coalition that can actually deliver.

Cross-University Coordination Networks

Building and maintaining the connective tissue between research institutions. When a technique exists in one lab and the need exists in another, the connection is not automatic. It requires intentional infrastructure: shared access agreements, data management protocols, technical staff career paths, and the trust that comes from repeated collaboration. The network itself is the infrastructure.

Lab Operations in the AI Era

Managing the operational layer where autonomous instrumentation, AI-driven analysis pipelines, and human researchers intersect. This means procurement of advanced microscopy and robotics systems, integration of machine learning workflows into experimental design, and the quiet redesign of what a lab technician's career looks like when half the bench work is done by models. The equipment is the easy part; the human and organisational adaptation is the real work.
