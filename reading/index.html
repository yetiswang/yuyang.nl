Reading

Library

A working library, lightly annotated. The substrate from which the public thinking comes: management, philosophy, the systems we work inside. What I read here ends up, eventually, in the essays. The diaries stay private.

The shape of the library

560 books across twenty-one domains. Most live in the reference catalog: science books I keep within reach for work, not finished reads. The active reading is concentrated elsewhere — strategy, economics, systems thinking, philosophy, statecraft. I read in English and Traditional Chinese; the two registers sit beside each other on the same shelf.

Not entertainment. Not novelty. Books that earn rereading.

Currently shaping my thinking

  • Ray Dalio — Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order. Long-arc framework for reading the moment.
  • Donella Meadows — Thinking in Systems. The book I return to when designing institutional architecture.
  • Daron Acemoglu & James Robinson — Why Nations Fail (國家為什麼會失敗). The inclusive-extractive frame keeps showing up in unexpected places.
  • Peter Drucker — Management and the Boxed Set. Still the cleanest writing on what management actually is.
  • Herminia Ibarra — Working Identity. On vocational change as practice, not insight.
  • 詹姆斯·霍利斯 — 中年之路 / 覺醒中年. Hollis on the second adulthood. Slow and serious.
  • 錢穆 — 中國歷代政治得失. A reread, to remember how political institutions actually evolve.

Currently reading

Lee Kuan Yew, Kissinger (World Order, On China), Jay Forrester (World Dynamics, Principles of Systems), Yuval Harari (Nexus), Daron Acemoglu (Power and Progress), and a stack of leadership and strategy titles. The mix shifts.

Recent papers

A few I have been tracking from the AI-driven discovery and self-driving lab literature:

  • Mitchener et al. — Kosmos: An AI Scientist for Autonomous Discovery (Edison Scientific, 2026). First AI-scientist system to credibly demonstrate cross-domain discovery, including a materials-science result. Sets a real benchmark.
  • Ghareeb et al. — Robin: Multi-Agent Autonomous Discovery (Nature 2026). Biology-focused counterpart to Kosmos. The two together mark the line where multi-agent discovery moves from preprint to peer-reviewed.
  • Vriza et al. — Agents on the Job: AutoGen at the X-ray Nanoprobe (npj Comp Mat, Argonne 2026). LLM-orchestrated lab on an actual user-facility instrument. Peer-reviewed evidence this is more than a demo.
  • Slautin & Kalinin — LLM Open-Hypothesis SPM (2026). Asks the harder question of whether language models can generate genuinely novel scientific hypotheses, not just operate instruments.
  • Moreno et al. — Modular Reconfigurable Lab Automation (2026). The engineering side of the same shift — what physical infrastructure actually enables interchangeable workflows.

Why this page exists

I am not trying to keep a comprehensive list. The vault holds 560 books and growing; this page holds the ones I would actually mention if you asked, alongside the recent papers I am tracking. Together they show the reading life that feeds the essays. Updated occasionally.
